Method, limits and sources

The two methods answer different questions

Snook / Liberty Mutual is numeric and force-based: how much force can a given percentage of the population exert, at this height, distance and frequency? Its answer is a design goal and a Risk Index. RAPP is qualitative and task-based: how many risk factors does this task have, and how bad is each? Its answer is a score per factor and a total used only to prioritise fixes. L23 is a separate force guideline shown inside the UK card; it is not part of the RAPP total. There is no combined score.

Snook notes

The design goal never depends on the measured force; only the Risk Index does. A lighter measured force does not raise the goal. Designing for more than 75% of the female work population offers the best protection; tasks acceptable to under 10% should be prioritised for redesign. The population percentages are for designing jobs so as many workers as possible can do them — not for deciding whether men or women can perform a job and placing workers accordingly. The tables apply to single tasks; for combined tasks, use the force corresponding to the smallest population percentage across the combined tasks. Some table values exceed the 8-hour physiological criteria in the source — a force within the Risk Index goal may still be too high for a full shift.

There is no OSHA maximum push/pull force. OSHA's eTool suggests keeping pushing forces below 50 lb — guidance, not a legal limit.

UK notes

Pushing and pulling is manual handling under the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992. Regulation 4 sets a hierarchy: avoid the handling where reasonably practicable → assess what cannot be avoided → reduce the risk to the lowest level reasonably practicable. There is no UK legal maximum weight or force for pushing/pulling. RAPP is guidance that helps meet those duties; it is not itself law, and using RAPP alone may not comprise a full risk assessment. HSE's three steps are: the L23 Appendix 1 simple posture filter → a RAPP detailed assessment → a full Schedule 1 risk assessment with the HSE checklist.

RAPP does not cover pushing or pulling with just the upper limbs (eg pushing buttons or levers, or loads on a conveyor), or just the lower limbs (eg pedals), or powered handling equipment, or handling people. For those, use a full Schedule 1 risk assessment instead — do not use a RAPP result. For lifting or carrying, use the MAC tool / lifting calculator instead.

The L23 guideline forces assume the force is applied with the hands between knuckle and shoulder height and over a distance of no more than about 20 m; if either is exceeded, a more detailed assessment is required. L23 expresses the guidelines in kg and rounds to about 10 N per kg; this app converts forces at 1 kg-force = 9.81 N, so its Newton figures can differ slightly from L23's rounded ones.

Jobs whose rate changes during the shift

One run of this app takes one frequency. For a job that changes rate: run the app once per rate, with the load and geometry identical in every run; in every run enter the same total minutes actually pushing/pulling for the whole shift (the sum across all rates). The governing result is the lowest design goal / highest Risk Index across the runs — normally the fastest rate. Never average the rates into a single frequency, and never add the runs' scheduled-repetition figures together — count total repetitions manually: rate × minutes at that rate, summed across rates. Frequency is the rate while pushing/pulling: four pushes in an hour done back-to-back at 1 per minute are entered as 1 per minute; the same four pushes spaced one every 15 minutes have a different, higher design goal. For RAPP, assess a task whose rate changes at its most repetitive work pattern.
